## Challenge

In 2014 Rostov-on-Don was preparing for the 2018 FIFA World Cup. The city's first plans for cycling were recreational paths on the outskirts. Such paths would not help people who wanted to ride to work, to study or around the centre.

The question was different: could cycling become part of the city's transport system? To answer it, the city needed facts about who rides, where and why.

## What we did

Urban Factory worked on the research and the concept. The research ran before and during Urban Festival 2014:

- surveys of cyclists;
- analysis of the city map and of real routes, based on GPS tracks shared by riders;
- interviews with bike rental operators, designers, cyclists, urban planners, transport specialists, local business and the city transport department.

At the festival, a workshop and a round table brought all these groups to one table.
The result was a concept document. It described a network of cycle lanes for different users: rental customers and tourists, leisure riders, and people commuting to work and study. The key idea was simple: cycle lanes are transport infrastructure, not recreation. The concept followed three steps: collect real bike tracks, join them into one network, then build it.

## Result

The concept moved through the full chain from research to construction:

1. research;
2. a city-wide cycling concept;
3. tender documentation for design, prepared by the city transport department on the basis of the concept;
4. design, carried out by a team that had taken part in the research;
5. construction of cycle lanes a few years later.

For Urban Factory, I Bike RnD showed that research done with the people who use the street can shape a city decision. Here it led to a design brief and, in the end, to lanes on the ground.
